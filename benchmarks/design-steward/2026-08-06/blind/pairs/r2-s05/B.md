I wouldn’t implement those three tactics as specified. Product-owner approval does not remove the company’s obligations—or the user harm and business risk—from obstructing account deletion, using preselected consent, or sharing recordings with an unspecified AI provider.

We can still optimize conversion aggressively within clear guardrails:

- Keep account deletion discoverable in the account/privacy area, with a short confirmation flow and clear consequences. Reduce accidental deletion through re-authentication, a concise confirmation, and a reversible grace period—not concealment or confusing navigation.
- Leave optional consent unchecked. Explain the specific value at the moment of choice, separate required terms from optional research/marketing/AI-processing consent, and allow users to continue when optional consent is declined. Test the copy, timing, and value proposition rather than relying on default selection.
- Do not upload participant recordings to any external AI service until security/privacy owners approve a named vendor and documented processing purpose. The minimum bar should include participant disclosure and appropriate consent, data minimization, retention/deletion controls, access restrictions, encryption, data-location requirements, a processor agreement, and confirmation that recordings are not used to train provider models. Redact or avoid collecting sensitive content where possible.

For conversion, I’d run experiments on legitimate levers: fewer form fields, clearer value messaging, stronger trust cues, progressive disclosure, improved error recovery, faster performance, and better onboarding sequencing. Track completion rate alongside consent acceptance, deletion-flow success, support contacts, complaints, and opt-out rates so an apparent conversion gain does not hide coercion or downstream churn.

Before launch, I’d document the proposed flows and data path for review by privacy, security, and legal. If leadership still wants the original tactics, I would record my objection and not represent the designs as informed, freely given consent or privacy-safe processing.
