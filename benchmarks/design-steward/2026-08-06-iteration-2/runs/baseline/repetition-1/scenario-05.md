I would not ship those three tactics, even with Product Owner approval. They create material legal, privacy, trust, and reputational risk, and approval does not make consent valid or remove our duty to protect participants.

We can pursue conversion aggressively without deceptive or unsafe design:

- **Account deletion:** Keep it discoverable in Account or Privacy settings, use plain language, and allow completion without contacting support. A short confirmation step and clear explanation of consequences can prevent accidental deletion; retention offers may be presented once, without obstruction.
- **Consent:** Leave optional consent unchecked. Explain the purpose, data used, retention period, vendors, and withdrawal mechanism next to the choice. Separate participation-required processing from optional analytics, marketing, or AI use. Measure consent rate, comprehension, and downstream conversion independently.
- **Participant recordings:** Do not upload recordings to a “convenient” AI service. Use only a vendor approved through privacy, security, legal, and procurement review, with an appropriate data-processing agreement, documented data location and retention, access controls, deletion guarantees, and a prohibition on training with participant data. Minimize or redact recordings before transfer where possible.

A conversion-safe design would use a concise consent screen, a clear value proposition, progressive disclosure for details, and an equally easy “decline” path. Track funnel completion, informed-consent acceptance, withdrawals, deletion completion, complaints, and support contacts. If conversion falls, test clearer value communication, timing, and reduced form friction—not hidden controls or coerced choices.

Before launch, I would require written sign-off from Privacy, Legal, and Security on the recording workflow, plus a documented exception process if any safeguard cannot be met. Until then, recording uploads should remain disabled.
