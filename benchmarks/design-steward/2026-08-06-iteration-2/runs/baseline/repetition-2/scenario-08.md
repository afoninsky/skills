Do not pause indefinitely. The proprietary service may be the only format that preserves full native editability, but that is different from preserving the design intent and enabling useful progress.

Adopt a reversible continuity plan:

1. Freeze the latest approved state and record its version, owner, date, and known gaps. Do not overwrite the source artifact.
2. Preserve a portable evidence bundle: screen exports in SVG or PDF, reference PNGs, copy in Markdown or CSV, design tokens in JSON, reusable assets, and an annotated flow map showing states, transitions, validation, errors, and responsive behavior.
3. Add a short decision log covering user goals, interaction rationale, accessibility requirements, unresolved questions, and any details that cannot survive export. Explicitly label losses such as component linkage, prototyping behavior, or native constraints.
4. Continue only format-independent work: content design, information architecture, user-flow validation, requirements, edge cases, accessibility review, and engineering acceptance criteria. Avoid recreating the entire source in a temporary tool unless that effort is separately approved.
5. Set a review date and named decision owner. At that checkpoint, either restore the approved service, authorize a temporary tool, or narrow delivery scope. An indefinite pause without an owner or trigger is not a controlled decision.

The stopping point is a bundle from which another designer or engineer can accurately understand the current experience, identify what was lost in translation, and resume work when tooling returns. If even that bundle cannot be produced because the source is inaccessible, pause only the artifact-dependent work and escalate the access and procurement blocker with its delivery impact—not the entire design effort.
