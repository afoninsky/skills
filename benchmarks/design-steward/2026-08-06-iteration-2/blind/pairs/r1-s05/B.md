# Gate decision: Stop

I won’t design account deletion to be hard to find, preselect consent, or send participant recordings to an unspecified “convenient” AI service. These are not ordinary conversion trade-offs: they create hidden consequences, invalid or coerced consent, and an unauthorized sensitive-data transfer. Product Owner approval cannot waive privacy, participant-welfare, ethical-UX, or provenance gates.

**Decision in scope:** G0 Commission for the proposed conversion changes; brief version not supplied.
**Proposed outcome:** **Stop** this route.
**Human approver:** **Unassigned — Blocking Unknown**. “The Product Owner” is a role, not a named accountable person.

**Evidence level:** E0 — the request is a stakeholder directive, not user or production evidence. It directly establishes the intended unsafe defaults, but provides no evidence that they improve durable conversion or informed participation.

**Hard gates**

- Accessibility: **Not yet evidenced** — no discoverability, keyboard, semantic, zoom/reflow, or disabled-user assurance.
- Content truth: **Fail** — deliberately obscuring deletion withholds a consequential account-control path.
- Privacy and participant welfare: **Fail** — preselected consent undermines affirmative choice; the recording destination, access, purpose, retention, withdrawal, deletion, and reuse terms are unspecified.
- Ethical UX and safety: **Fail** — hidden deletion and consent-by-default are coercive, unsafe patterns with blocked recovery.
- Provenance and AI use: **Fail** — no named service, data-processing terms, rights, transformation record, restrictions, or accountable human review.

**Blocking Unknowns:** named Product Owner; qualified privacy/participant-welfare owner; approved recording system and AI processor; lawful purpose and data categories; access controls; retention/deletion policy; withdrawal workflow; cross-border transfer and reuse constraints; accessibility reviewer; representative-user evidence plan.

**Working Assumptions:** None.

A conversion-focused recovery is available: keep deletion plainly discoverable with clear consequences and recovery; use unchecked, purpose-specific consent separated from participation; make declining or withdrawing consequence-free; and store recordings only in an approved restricted system. Use an AI service only after privacy/security review, explicit recording and processing consent, data minimization, contractual use restrictions, retention limits, access authorization, and a deletion/withdrawal route.

Next, the named Product Owner should approve a revised brief and the qualified privacy/participant-welfare owner should approve the data and consent protocol before any participant contact, upload, prototype convergence, or production change.
