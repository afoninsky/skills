## Shared comparison contract

For a substantial commission, default to **12–15 raw concepts → 6 territories → 3 developed directions → 1 backbone**. This is a staged investment rule, not an artifact quota. For a genuinely narrow commission, reduce the funnel only when the recorded rationale shows that the default would add cost without improving the decision. Every presented direction must independently meet the professional quality floor; replace weak work instead of presenting filler.

Freeze a shared baseline before work begins:

- approved brief version and success contract;
- evidence, requirement, role, domain, journey, and handoff IDs;
- product experience thesis and solution-neutral structure;
- representative content and decisive, hostile, waiting, failure, and recovery states;
- Fixed and Challengeable constraints;
- professional-quality criteria, hard gates, and comparison rubric;
- evidence thresholds and uncertainty handling;
- artifact, iteration, time, and delegation budget;
- six distinct territory definitions and, after selection, one structural and experiential charter per developed direction.

Also freeze the first-visible-artifact target, feedback checkpoint plan, preselection revision limit, checks due at each fidelity, and checks deferred until selection under [rapid-design-loop.md](rapid-design-loop.md).

The comparison rubric must reward the commissioned outcome. Do not overweight traceability, number of states, novelty, or implementation detail. Include product specificity, first-use clarity, interaction coherence, visual and content craft, responsive quality, ecosystem coverage, and the user's stated priorities.

## Direction funnel

Do not jump from a confirmed brief to several complete coded prototypes.

1. **Generate 12–15 raw concepts privately.** Keep each to one short paragraph with the product thesis and core user act. Vary the mental model, value proposition, structure, or interaction—not merely palette, typography, or layout. Do not write code, detailed UI specifications, or durable records.
2. **Eliminate before making.** Remove ideas that are generic, structurally redundant, implausible, scope-incomplete, constraint-breaking, or dependent on unavailable capabilities. Preserve only one-line rejection reasons in working notes.
3. **Form six territories.** Cluster the strongest concepts into six materially distinct territories. Give each an experience promise, core act, first-ten-seconds hierarchy, mental model, interaction grammar, visual territory, content voice, and anti-goals.
4. **Expose visual thinking cheaply.** Produce one matched low-fidelity visual or interaction sketch per territory with representative content and show a six-up contact sheet. Do not build six prototypes, design systems, or exhaustive direction records.
5. **Select three developed directions.** Use professional judgment against the confirmed brief, evidence, coverage, risk, and quality floor. Freeze one charter per selected direction with an ownable constellation and decisive journey slice.
6. **Render before implementing.** Produce one realistic representative frame per direction, inspect screenshots before rationales or code, and reject or revise weak work. A representative desktop frame is enough for this checkpoint.
7. **Build only surviving proof.** Develop the core act and immediate consequence only for Promising or Strong directions. Revise at most twice before promoting, pivoting, or rejecting.
8. **Converge on one backbone.** Expand the strongest coherent direction or approved synthesis to representative mobile behavior, one material recovery state, and G4 evidence. Spend deeper implementation and assurance effort only after this convergence.

When a System Owner rejects a round, return to the failed thesis, coverage assumption, quality criteria, or structure. Do not increase direction count, prototype breadth, agent count, or documentation volume as a substitute for diagnosis.

## Rapid visible checkpoints

Follow [rapid-design-loop.md](rapid-design-loop.md). A visible checkpoint is progress feedback, not a new human gate. Once G1/G3 authorize generation, target the first useful frame within 15 minutes of active work or 15% of the generation budget and the first comparable contact sheet before 25%.

Spend at least half of the preselection design budget on rendered creation, inspection, and revision. If research, records, delegation packets, audits, or testing consume more time than visible iteration, stop and defer work that cannot change the current promote/pivot/reject decision.

Do not run an independent G1 audit by default. Do not add pairwise direction critics. The default preselection topology keeps the entire funnel with the Steward; isolated developed-direction authors or one non-author critic require a named decision-relevant independence need.

## Authorship and optional independence

Keep raw concepts, territory formation, selection, and synthesis with the Steward. Do not assign one agent per concept or territory. A broad funnel needs breadth of ideas, not breadth of delegation.

The Steward may author all three developed directions when independent authorship is not evidence needed for the decision. Freeze the three charters before detailed rendering to reduce anchoring, and make no claim that their authorship was independent.

Use one fresh, history-free sub-agent per developed direction only when a named comparison risk, benchmark condition, or commission requirement makes isolated authorship materially valuable. Give every author the same baseline and only its charter; never reuse an author for a sibling direction or expose sibling solutions before freeze. Dispatch in parallel only when runtime evidence proves overlap. If isolated authorship was required but unavailable, record the limitation and mark only that independence claim **Not yet evidenced**; do not block unrelated Steward-authored exploration.

Keep an author attached to its direction through bounded revision. Stop after two preselection revisions unless the brief records another cap. Use one living delegation ledger when delegation occurs.

When visual development warrants the verified `frontend-design` specialist, use a bounded specialist sub-agent under [specialist-capabilities.md](specialist-capabilities.md). Supply sufficient product essence, emotional intent, audience, decisive journey, coverage, content, assets, and precedent territory. Do not starve specialist context in the name of isolation.

Make charters structurally and experientially distinct. Examples include task-first versus object-first navigation, guided succession versus direct manipulation, progressive disclosure versus overview-first, or contextual versus centralized control. A direction also needs a distinct first-ten-seconds hierarchy, interaction grammar, and art direction. Color, typography, spacing, and ornament alone do not create an alternative.

## Precedent and art direction

Use high-trust primary or first-party sources when citing professional precedent. When browsing or asset retrieval is authorized, use it deliberately to build a small, rights-aware territory rather than relying on model memory.

Record:

- stable source and observed date;
- original context and intended population;
- principle abstracted from the source;
- rights or license considerations;
- transformation into the current direction;
- limitations and reasons the precedent may not transfer.

Use several principles or sources where possible so a direction is transformed rather than copied. Do not reproduce a surface, brand language, proprietary asset, or interaction merely because it is familiar.

Do not impose a system-font-only, no-image, native-control, or dependency-free constraint for convenience. Those are legitimate constraints only when required by the brief, environment, rights, portability, fidelity, or decision risk. When they apply, demand a deliberate visual system rather than accepting raw defaults.

## Fidelity and artifact presentation

Choose the lowest fidelity that can answer the current risk:

| Question | Preferred artifact | Portable review form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journey, dependency, state, or information structure | Mermaid source | SVG or PDF |
| Rough spatial or interaction alternative | Excalidraw JSON or compact HTML | SVG or screenshots |
| Visual territory and layout silhouette | Concept board, static frames, or thin coded slice | Side-by-side images |
| Shared editable interface design | Penpot when open/self-hosted control matters; Figma when explicitly authorized and organizationally standard | Structured export plus static review |
| Responsive behavior, keyboard flow, semantics, motion, or feasibility | Disposable HTML/CSS/JS; add Storybook or Playwright when useful | Runnable local artifact plus captures or test output |
| Decision and approval surface | Markdown or HTML | Stable Markdown, HTML, or PDF |

Every frozen or decision-bearing artifact must include, in its manifest or review wrapper rather than inside the product viewport:

- stable artifact ID and version;
- decision question and intended audience;
- linked brief, requirement, evidence, assumption, direction, and gate IDs;
- editable source and portable review/export;
- provenance, rights, and tool/AI disclosure;
- fidelity limitations and unsupported claims;
- open fallback or migration route.

Separate the review harness from the product surface. State selectors, scenario navigation, artifact IDs, provenance warnings, rationale links, hashes, and audit controls belong in a review index, browser frame, or adjacent panel that cannot be mistaken for product UI.

Treat tool names as replaceable implementation choices. Never make a service account or proprietary format the sole durable record.

## Quality, sameness, and comparison

Before any non-author critique:

1. remove obvious failures through the frame and thin-slice loop;
2. freeze each surviving direction's thesis, content, decisive journey, concise rationale, screenshots, and artifact hash;
3. verify baseline parity and experience-coverage parity;
4. inspect a desktop/mobile contact sheet before reading rationales;
5. run every counterfactual in [design-quality.md](design-quality.md), including relabel, default-cluster, silhouette, core-act, first-ten-seconds, craft, and family-system tests;
6. revise, replace, or reject directions that are generic, unfinished, scope-incomplete, or merely themed variants.

After the Steward quality pass, use a fresh critic only when adversarial independence or specialist judgment is material to the decision. The critic must have authored none of the directions. Give the critic frozen screenshots first, then artifacts, baseline, charters, quality criteria, hard gates, and rubric. Require concrete observations and a verdict for each direction. Do not outsource selection authority or use critique as confident self-scoring.

Obtain additional critique only when a named material risk requires qualified specialist judgment. Keep each original thesis visible when requesting revisions and route every requested change back through the Steward and original author. Do not create pairwise critics or separate recheck agents for ordinary visual iteration.

Professional quality is a G4 readiness floor. A direction that is generic, incoherent, unfinished, or materially incomplete does not enter scoring. Evaluate hard gates before comparison and disqualify unresolved critical failures in accessibility, content truth, privacy or participant welfare, ethical UX or safety, or provenance.

Compare surviving directions against the precommitted rubric. Separate observed evidence, generated design judgment, qualified specialist judgment, System Owner preference, implementation estimate, and Steward inference. Prefer anchored qualitative trade-offs. If numeric scoring is required, avoid false precision, calibrate the scale before judging, and report evaluator disagreement.

## Synthesis

Choose one coherent structural backbone. Import an element from another direction only when:

- it addresses a documented weakness or requirement;
- it is compatible with the backbone's mental model, interaction grammar, content voice, and art direction;
- evidence or specialist reasoning supports it;
- its dependencies and consequences are recorded;
- the resulting synthesis is retested against the quality floor, hard gates, and rubric.

Do not average, vote, collage, or include one element from each direction for representational fairness.

## Prototype boundary

Mark prototype code as disposable design evidence. Keep it outside production modules and credentials. Do not silently promote it into production. End design handoff with an implementation contract that describes intended behavior, semantics, content and data rules, responsive states, accessibility intent, instrumentation, acceptance criteria, known limitations, and the visual/interaction qualities production must preserve.
